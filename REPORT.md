# Lua wins the boot-versus-throughput tradeoff in Rust

For a Rust host, **boot cost is mostly a non-issue and throughput is where engines split by two orders of magnitude.** On an Apple M5, a native Rust hello-world process takes 1.25 ms. Lua 5.4/5.5, LuaJIT, rquickjs (QuickJS-NG), wasmi and Rhai all add only **0.07 to 0.32 ms** on top of that. Starlark, Rune, uncached wasmtime and CPython add 1.5 to 6.7 ms. Steel adds **87 ms**, which disqualifies it for CLIs. Steady-state speed is a different story: on recursive `fib(30)` the spread runs from wasmtime at 3 ms to Rhai at **618 ms**, about 200x. The practical upshot is that **mlua (PUC Lua or LuaJIT) is the only family that sits near the top on both axes at once.** wasmtime is the throughput king once compile cost is moved off the hot path, and Rhai (benchmarked here on its default AST interpreter, *not* the new Grain bytecode VM) buys ergonomics and sandboxing at a 12 to 30x speed penalty versus Lua. Everything below separates **local measurements** (Apple M5, 2026-10-27) from **cited third-party numbers**. The two agree on ordering wherever they overlap.

## The candidates fall into three families with very different costs

**Bound C/C++ runtimes** are the mature core. **mlua** covers Lua 5.1 through 5.5, LuaJIT and Luau (with optional native codegen) behind one API, ships `async`, memory limits and hooks, and vendors the C build so cross-compiling is painless ([mlua README](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua)). It replaced rlua, which is now a deprecated re-export ([rlua README](https://github.com/mlua-rs/rlua)). **rquickjs** binds QuickJS-NG, the community fork of Bellard's QuickJS, and maps Promises to Rust futures ([rquickjs README](https://github.com/DelSkayn/rquickjs)). **PyO3** embeds CPython. **deno_core/rusty_v8** gives you real V8, but the prebuilt static library alone is **38 MiB gzipped** for aarch64 macOS ([rusty_v8 v152.2.0](https://github.com/denoland/rusty_v8/releases/tag/v152.2.0)). Bindings for Wren, Janet, mruby, Squirrel, Guile and Tcl all show under ~3k downloads in the last 90 days or haven't published since 2019 to 2024, so treat them as hobby-grade ([crates.io API](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/mlua)).

**Pure-Rust languages** trade speed for a Rust-native toolchain and nicer embedding. **Rhai**, **Boa** (JavaScript) and **starlark-rust** (Meta's Buck2 implementation) are the high-volume, actively released ones. **Rune**, **Koto** and **Steel** (Scheme) are alive but pre-1.0 ([crates.io data](https://crates.io/crates/rhai)). **Roto** stands out: it's statically typed and Cranelift-JIT-compiled ([Roto README](https://codeberg.org/NLnetLabs/roto)). **Mun** hasn't released since December 2022 and its compiler needs LLVM 14. **Piccolo** hasn't released since June 2024 ([mun](https://github.com/mun-lang/mun), [piccolo](https://github.com/kyren/piccolo)). Rhai 1.27.0 (2026-10-21) declared its **Grain bytecode VM "no longer experimental"** and at feature parity with the AST interpreter. Grain sits behind the opt-in `grain` feature, and a `no_ast` build runs only precompiled bytecode ([Rhai CHANGELOG](https://github.com/rhaiscript/rhai/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)).

**WebAssembly runtimes** turn "scripting" into "plugins compiled from another language". **wasmtime** has Cranelift (optimizing), Winch (baseline) and Pulley (portable interpreter) tiers ([Wasmtime docs](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/examples-fast-compilation.html)). **wasmi** 2.0 (2026-09-01) is the leading pure-Rust Wasm interpreter, about 2.2x faster than 1.0, with stable fuel metering ([Wasmi 2.0 blog](https://wasmi-labs.github.io/blog/posts/wasmi-v2.0/)). Zed, Zellij and SWC/Next.js all host plugins on wasmtime ([Zed](https://zed.dev/blog/zed-decoded-extensions), [Next.js commit](https://app.semanticdiff.com/gh/vercel/next.js/commit/a24462d9e5244f78679383e6aa41e107dd9520ac)). The catch is developer experience: users have to compile a guest, so there's no edit-and-run loop.

## Local benchmarks: boot is a 0.3 ms story for most engines, and an 87 ms one for Steel

All tables in this section are **local measurements**: Apple M5 (4P+6E), macOS 26.6.2, rustc 1.96.0, release builds with no LTO, median of 3 passes. Process timings come from `hyperfine -N --warmup 5 --runs 50`. The harness is this repo; raw output is in `results/` and full methodology in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md). Versions: mlua 0.12.2 (Lua 5.4.9, 5.5.1, LuaJIT 2.1, Luau 0.740), rhai 1.27.0, rquickjs 0.14.0, boa_engine 0.22.0, rune 0.14.2, koto 0.16.1, wasmtime 49.0.4 (50.0 needs rustc 1.97), wasmi 2.0.0, pyo3 0.30.1 with CPython 3.13.14, starlark 0.14.2, steel-core 0.8.3. V8/deno_core was excluded.

Three findings here aren't obvious from the docs. First, **in-process microbenchmarks understate cold cost.** Starlark creates an engine in 3.8 µs inside a warm loop, yet its process costs 1.45 ms more than native. Koto and Luau show the same gap: under 45 µs warm, 0.91 ms cold. The process number captures page-ins, lazy statics and first-touch allocation that a warm loop amortizes away, so **for CLIs, trust the hyperfine column.** Second, **the slow-boot outliers each have one fixable cause.** Rune spends 1.45 ms of its 1.55 ms in `Context::with_default_modules()`, and with a reused Context compile+call drops to **6.8 µs**. wasmtime spends its time in Cranelift: compiling a 294-byte module costs 258 µs, `Module::deserialize` of a `.cwasm` costs 19 µs, and `InstancePre::instantiate` costs **0.7 µs**. Steel's 57 ms goes to compiling its Scheme prelude at engine creation, and even the `new_base` variant still costs 25 ms. Third, CPython can initialize only once per process, so it can't hand out cheap per-request isolates the way Lua states or QuickJS contexts can.

The cited numbers line up. Rhai documents that `Engine::new_raw()` plus `StandardPackage::new_lazy()` builds the stdlib **~8x faster with ~16x less heap** than `Engine::new()` ([Rhai CHANGELOG](https://github.com/rhaiscript/rhai/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). Locally, a bare `new_raw` came in at 0.54 µs, though that's a floor, since the optimizer probably constant-folds `1+1`. Upstream QuickJS claims a full runtime life cycle in under 300 µs, and we measured 45 µs ([rquickjs README](https://github.com/DelSkayn/rquickjs)). For V8, the V8 team says snapshots cut context creation "from 40 ms down to less than 2 ms" ([V8 blog](https://v8.dev/blog/custom-startup-snapshots)), and `deno eval 0` measured 11.4 ms per process on the same M5, per the C-runtime research pass. That puts V8 in CPython's boot tier, with a binary 10x bigger.

The tiers hold across workloads. **JIT tier:** wasmtime and LuaJIT, at 5 to 7x faster than PUC Lua on compute. **Fast interpreters:** wasmi, the Lua family, Luau. **Mid tier:** QuickJS, CPython, Starlark, Rune, Steel, at 2 to 5x slower than Lua. **Slow tier:** Boa, Koto, Rhai-AST, at 6 to 30x slower. Some details matter more than the averages. **Lua 5.5 nearly halved 5.4's loop cost** (1.77 vs 3.02 ns/iter) and is otherwise on par, so it's a free upgrade. **Luau's native codegen only helped recursion** (fib 36 → 18 ms) and did nothing for loops, strings or host calls, while costing +37 µs boot and +0.6 MB. That matches Roblox's statement that codegen targets number- and array-heavy code, not API-call-dominated code ([Luau Recap Oct 2023](https://luau.org/news/2023-11-01-luau-recap-october-2023)). **String building compresses the field**: CPython and Starlark beat PUC Lua, because the work happens in native join code rather than the dispatch loop. **Starlark has the cheapest host calls of any interpreter** (10.9 ns), which matters more than its fib time for config-style scripts that mostly call into Rust.

The one maintained cross-engine suite, **script-bench-rs** (Apple M5 Max, refreshed 2026-10-03), sorts 10,000 Rust userdata objects with in-script quicksort and Rust-side comparisons. It reports: wasmtime 2.71 ms, wasmi 7.04, Luau 11.82, **Roto 12.04**, Lua 5.5 15.47, rquickjs 36.38, Koto 49.33, Boa 79.99, **Rhai 112.02** ([script-bench-rs](https://github.com/khvzak/script-bench-rs)). Its order matches ours with one exception. **Luau beats Lua 5.5 there** but loses to PUC Lua on our fib, which says Luau's edge is in table- and interop-heavy code rather than raw call dispatch. Our run also used mlua's default `optimization_level: 1`, which could leave some Luau performance on the table. script-bench-rs adds **Roto**, which we didn't measure. It runs at Luau speed, making it the only pure-Rust language in the fast tier, but it pays Cranelift compile on load and requires static typing.

**Grain won't rescue Rhai's ranking.** Rhai's own benchmarks put Grain at 1.2 s on recursive Fibonacci versus 3.2 s for the full AST build, and 0.05 s versus 0.2 s on a 1M loop. On primes, though, Grain was *slower* than the performance-tuned AST build (1.1 vs 0.85 s) ([Rhai benchmarks](https://rhai.rs/book/about/benchmarks.html)). Apply a generous 2.5x to our 618 ms fib and Rhai lands at ~250 ms, level with Boa and Koto and still ~12x behind PUC Lua. That's an extrapolation, not a measurement. The cited Grain speedups also come from a different machine and an unstated version.

## Short-lived versus long-running: boot cost amortizes fast, and throughput gaps don't

For a short-lived CLI, boot differences among the good cluster (Lua, LuaJIT, rquickjs, wasmi, Rhai) are **at most 0.25 ms**. That's roughly what Rhai's AST interpreter spends on **7,000 loop iterations**, or what PUC Lua spends on 80,000. So once a CLI script does more than trivial work, interpreter speed outweighs boot, even for a command that lives 5 ms. The exceptions are the outliers. **Steel's 87 ms boot equals four full `fib(30)` runs in PUC Lua**, so Steel loses every short-lived comparison no matter what the script does. CPython's +6.65 ms is a full order of magnitude above the cluster. Rune is fine only if you can avoid rebuilding the default Context per process, which a CLI can't. **wasmtime is the interesting crossover.** Compiling even a 294-byte module adds ~1.4 ms per process over loading a precompiled `.cwasm`, and real modules scale up from there: an informal Extism benchmark saw 32 ms per run compiled versus 10 ms precompiled ([extism-bench](https://git.batsense.net/realaravinth/extism-bench)). A CLI using wasmtime should ship or cache `.cwasm` artifacts via `Config::cache` or precompile at install ([Wasmtime docs](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/examples-fast-compilation.html)). Otherwise wasmi wins any script that finishes in under a few hundred microseconds of compute. Locally the breakeven between wasmi and uncached wasmtime was **~0.33 ms of wasmi execution** for our tiny module.

For long-running hosts, boot falls out of the equation and two other factors take over: per-call interop cost and isolation. A server instantiating per request should look at **wasmtime with `InstancePre`** (0.7 µs locally). The canonical published figure is SpiderMonkey.wasm instantiation dropping from ~2 ms to **5 µs** with copy-on-write heap images and lazy init ([Wasmtime 1.0 performance](https://bytecodealliance.org/articles/wasmtime-10-performance)), though the pooling allocator that gets you there is off by default ([PoolingAllocationConfig](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/wasmtime/struct.PoolingAllocationConfig.html)). Lua states at ~20 µs are cheap enough to create per request too. Async hosts have specific fits: **Rune** has first-class async with generators ([Rune README](https://github.com/rune-rs/rune)), mlua's `async` works with any executor, rquickjs converts Promises to futures, and deno_core runs natively on tokio ([mlua README](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua), [rquickjs README](https://github.com/DelSkayn/rquickjs)). Untrusted code narrows the field further. mlua's `Lua::sandbox` is **Luau-only** ([mlua README](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua)). Rhai offers operation counting via `on_progress`, Boa exposes an instruction budget, Rune ships a budget module ([Rhai CHANGELOG](https://github.com/rhaiscript/rhai/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), [Boa CHANGELOG](https://github.com/boa-dev/boa/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), [rune runtime](https://github.com/rune-rs/rune/tree/main/crates/rune/src/runtime)), and Wasm gives memory-safe isolation by construction, with deterministic fuel in wasmi ([Wasmi 2.0 blog](https://wasmi-labs.github.io/blog/posts/wasmi-v2.0/)). CPython has no in-process sandbox at all. Wasm sandboxes are only as strong as their tier, too: a Winch-only CVE (CVE-2026-34987) and a pooled-memory reuse advisory argue for the default Cranelift path in multi-tenant hosts ([CVE-2026-34987](https://releasealert.dev/cve/CVE-2026-34987), [advisory](https://advisories.ecosyste.ms/advisories/GSA_kwCzR0hTQS13aDZ3LTM4MjgtZzlxZs4AAv0W)).

## Interop: exposing Rust and reusing ecosystems

Speed decides which engines are viable. Interop decides which ones are pleasant to live with, and it splits the field differently. **Exposing Rust is a solved problem almost everywhere**: most engines now turn an annotated struct into a script class with a derive or attribute macro. **Reusing an existing library ecosystem is where the real differences are.** Only PyO3 gets a mainstream package registry wholesale. Lua gets one with caveats, and JS engines get npm only after bundling. The pure-Rust languages have no script ecosystem at all. Their "packages" are Rust crates the host compiles in. Unless marked otherwise, everything below is a **cited or code-read finding, not a measurement**. Detail and verification status live in `research_notes/…/interop.md`.

**Registering a function is a one-liner nearly everywhere, and registering a type costs a derive plus a few attributes.** The bench crates show the floor. mlua's `create_function(|_, (a, b): (i64, i64)| Ok(a + b))`, Rhai's `register_fn("add", |a: i64, b: i64| a + b)`, Rune's `m.function("add", …)`, Steel's `register_fn` and wasmtime/wasmi's `func_wrap` all take typed closures. Koto and Boa are the outliers. Koto's `add_fn` hands you an argument slice to pattern-match, and Boa's `NativeFunction` takes `(this, args, ctx)`. A struct with a field and a mutating method looks like this in the three most common choices:

```rust
// mlua: implement UserData by hand (or #[derive(UserData)] in 0.12)
impl UserData for Counter {
    fn add_methods<M: UserDataMethods<Self>>(m: &mut M) {
        m.add_method_mut("incr", |_, this, by: i64| { this.n += by; Ok(()) });
    }
}

// Rhai: derive the field accessors, register methods on the builder
#[derive(Clone, CustomType)]
struct Counter { n: i64 }
engine.build_type::<Counter>().register_fn("incr", Counter::incr);

// PyO3: attribute macros, then make the module importable before init
#[pyclass] struct Counter { #[pyo3(get, set)] n: i64 }
#[pymethods] impl Counter { fn incr(&mut self, by: i64) { self.n += by; } }
pyo3::append_to_inittab!(host);
```

These snippets are illustrative and weren't compiled. Starlark carries the most ceremony for a type. You implement `StarlarkValue<'v>` with `#[starlark_value]` and derive `ProvidesStaticType`, `NoSerialize` and `Allocative`, though `starlark_simple_value!` trims it for immutable values ([starlark docs](https://docs.rs/starlark/latest/starlark/)). Boa just gained `#[boa_class]` and `#[boa_module]`, so its old `Class`-trait boilerplate is now optional ([Boa docs](https://docs.rs/boa_engine/latest/boa_engine/)). **wasmtime flips the model**: you write the WIT interface, `bindgen!` generates a host trait per interface, and resources live as handles in a `ResourceTable`, so nothing crosses by reference ([`bindgen!`](https://docs.rs/wasmtime/latest/wasmtime/component/macro.bindgen.html)).

**Ownership rules are where the engines really differ.** Rhai copies values: custom types must be `Clone`, and shared mutation means `Rc<RefCell<…>>` or `Arc<Mutex<…>>` ([Rhai book](https://rhai.rs/book/rust/custom-types.html)). Roto has the same rule, plus `'static` and no generic types ([Roto README](https://codeberg.org/NLnetLabs/roto)). PyO3 forbids lifetimes and generics on `#[pyclass]`, requires `Send + Sync` (or `unsendable`, which panics off-thread), and borrow-checks at runtime with `PyRef`/`PyRefMut` ([PyO3 guide](https://pyo3.rs/main/class)). rquickjs and Starlark tie values to a context or heap lifetime (`'js`, `'v`). Escaping that lifetime needs `Persistent` in rquickjs, or freezing the module in Starlark. **mlua is the only engine here with a first-class scoped-borrow API.** `Lua::scope` lets scripts call into non-`'static`, non-`Send` Rust values and invalidates them when the scope returns ([mlua `Lua`](https://docs.rs/mlua/latest/mlua/struct.Lua.html)). One footgun stands out. Boa's `NativeFunction::from_closure` is `unsafe` because the GC can't trace captured values ([Boa `NativeFunction`](https://docs.rs/boa_engine/latest/boa_engine/native_function/struct.NativeFunction.html)).

**Async host functions split along the same line as the report's async picks.** mlua (`create_async_function`, any executor), rquickjs (Promise ↔ Future), Boa (`from_async_fn`), deno_core, Rune and wasmtime (`imports: { default: async }`) all support them. PyO3's `async fn` sits behind `experimental-async`, is asyncio-only and requires `Send + 'static` futures ([PyO3 async](https://pyo3.rs/main/async-await)). I found no async host-function API for Rhai, Koto, Starlark or Roto. wasmi has no async either, but its **resumable calls** let the host pause on a trap or fuel exhaustion and resume later ([wasmi docs](https://docs.rs/wasmi/latest/wasmi/)). Error propagation is uniform enough to skip: every engine maps a Rust `Result` into a script exception or trap (Rhai via `return_raw`, wasmtime via `trappable`).

**Reusing an ecosystem is a three-way split.**

- **CPython via PyO3 is the only route to a mainstream registry with native extensions.** `PyModule::import` reaches anything installed, NumPy included ([PyO3 guide](https://pyo3.rs/main/python-from-rust/calling-existing-code)). The cost is distribution. Dynamic linking means shipping libpython, and static embedding is UNIX-only, which "PyO3 does not yet have first-class support for" ([PyO3 distribution](https://pyo3.rs/main/building-and-distribution)). PyOxidizer hasn't released since December 2022 (community reports, not an official notice: [pants chat](https://chat.pantsbuild.org/t/16408126/status-of-pyozidizer-i-am-using-pyoxidizer-which-only-suppor)). The live building block is python-build-standalone, which moved to Astral in December 2024 and underpins uv and mise ([Szorc](https://gregoryszorc.com/blog/2024/12/03/transferring-python-build-standalone-stewardship-to-astral)). Together with the +6.65 ms boot and 17.5 MB libpython measured above, that makes PyO3 a deliberate "we need PyPI" choice, never a default.
- **PUC Lua and LuaJIT reuse LuaRocks with one sharp edge.** Pure-Lua rocks just work: the `package` library is in mlua's safe stdlib set, so `require` over `package.path` works on a plain `Lua::new()` ([`StdLib`](https://docs.rs/mlua/latest/mlua/struct.StdLib.html)). C rocks are harder. `Lua::new()` "will not allow to load … C modules", so you need `Lua::unsafe_new()` ([mlua `Lua`](https://docs.rs/mlua/latest/mlua/struct.Lua.html)). With the `vendored` feature, Lua is linked statically, and a `.so` then fails with `undefined symbol: lua_pushlstring` ([mlua#524](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua/issues/524)). The FAQ fix is `-C link-args=-rdynamic` to export the host's Lua symbols ([mlua FAQ](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua/blob/main/FAQ.md)). The rock still has to be built against the same Lua version. LuaJIT's FFI is behind the unsafe `StdLib::FFI`. mlua's **module mode** runs the other way: it builds your Rust as a `cdylib` that a stock `lua` loads with `require`, which is the right shape when Lua is the host and Rust is the library ([mlua README](https://github.com/mlua-rs/mlua)). **Luau gives this up on purpose.** It has no `package` library and no C modules, only a custom `require` through mlua's `Require` trait. Its package world (Lune as a standalone runtime, pesde and Wally as package managers) assumes Roblox or Lune host APIs ([lune](https://docs.rs/crate/lune/latest), [pesde](https://docs.rs/crate/pesde/latest)).
- **The JS engines get npm only through a bundler, and Node APIs not at all.** rquickjs and Boa have ES modules with pluggable resolvers and loaders, but no `node:fs`, `Buffer` or `process`. Pure-ESM packages bundled with esbuild or rollup work. Anything that touches Node built-ins needs a polyfill. For rquickjs, the community **AWS LLRT Modules** ("mimic some of the Node APIs in pure Rust") and **Rquickjs Extra** cover part of the gap ([rquickjs README](https://github.com/DelSkayn/rquickjs)). deno_core has the strongest path through `deno_node`, but an embedder has to implement npm resolution (`NpmResolver`) because the CLI's version isn't reusable ([Deno Q&A](https://questions.deno.com/m/1211890402165006437), community thread from 2024).

The pure-Rust languages ship stdlibs, not ecosystems. Rhai's "packages" are Rust crates (rhai-rand, rhai-fs, rhai-url, rhai-sci, rhai-ml, rhai-bigint) ([Rhai packages](https://rhai.rs/book/lib/index.html)). Rune has `rune-modules`, and Koto has its core and extra libs. Starlark is hermetic by design: `load()` only sees what your loader serves. **Steel is the exception with a real script-level ecosystem.** It's mostly R5RS-compliant with R7RS "underway", and ships a `forge` package manager plus Rust dylib modules ([Steel README](https://github.com/mattwparas/steel)). Wasm reuses *compiled-language* ecosystems instead. A Rust, Go, C or componentized Python/JS guest brings its own libraries, and Extism's PDKs make that easy across about a dozen languages ([Extism host functions](https://extism.org/docs/concepts/host-functions)). Every plugin still has to be built first.

**Typing the host API for script authors is uneven.** Rhai has the best built-in story: `Engine::definitions()` (features `metadata` + `internals`) dumps every registered function to definition files or JSON, and the Rhai language server consumes them ([Rhai definitions](https://rhai.rs/book/engine/metadata/definitions.html)). Starlark ships `starlark_lsp`, a linter and DAP ([starlark-rust](https://github.com/facebook/starlark-rust)). luau-lsp accepts custom definition files through `luau-lsp.types.definitionFiles`, but you write them by hand ([luau-lsp](https://github.com/JohnnyMorganz/luau-lsp)). PyO3 can generate `.pyi` stubs via `experimental-inspect`, still under active development ([PyO3 type stubs](https://pyo3.rs/main/type-stub)). In wasmtime, the WIT file *is* the typed contract. **I found no `.d.ts` generator for rquickjs or Boa host APIs**, so JS hosts hand-write their TypeScript declarations.

## Recommendations by use case

| Use case | Pick | Runner-up | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Short-lived CLI** (boot-dominated) | **mlua + Lua 5.5** (vendored): +0.17 ms, 0.75 MB, fast interpreter | LuaJIT if scripts compute (+0.27 ms, 5x faster); rquickjs if users expect JS | Steel (+87 ms), CPython (+6.7 ms), wasmtime without `.cwasm`, Rune |
| **Long-running app** (throughput-dominated) | **LuaJIT via mlua** for dynamic scripting | Luau (+codegen for numeric code) if you also need sandboxing; Rune if the host is async and you want Rust-flavored syntax | Rhai-AST, Koto, Boa for hot paths |
| **Untrusted / sandboxed user scripts** | **wasmtime** (Cranelift, epoch/fuel, pooling, `InstancePre`) for plugins, or **Luau via mlua** with `Lua::sandbox` for scripts | wasmi for deterministic fuel or `no_std`; Rhai for small expression-level user logic with op limits | CPython (no sandbox), PUC Lua or LuaJIT without OS isolation, and any mlua state built with `unsafe_new` to load C rocks |
| **Config-ish scripting** (mostly calls into Rust) | **starlark-rust**: deterministic and hermetic, cheapest interpreter host calls (10.9 ns), Python syntax | Rhai (Rust-like syntax, limits, try `new_raw` + lazy stdlib or Grain); Nickel if output is pure static config | Steel; CPython |
| **Max throughput** | **wasmtime + precompiled `.cwasm`**: 0.15x Lua on fib, near-native loop | LuaJIT for dynamic code; Roto (≈ Luau speed in script-bench-rs) if static typing fits a pure-Rust stack | Any tree-walking or slow-tier interpreter |
| **Scripts must reuse an existing library ecosystem** | **pyo3** if you need PyPI or native extensions; accept libpython + site-packages in the bundle and +6.7 ms boot | **PUC Lua or LuaJIT via mlua** for LuaRocks: pure-Lua rocks work out of the box, C rocks need `unsafe_new` + `-rdynamic`; rquickjs (+ LLRT modules) for bundled pure-ESM npm code | Luau (no C modules or `package`) and the pure-Rust languages, which have no third-party script ecosystem |

Interop changes one pick and sharpens another. When scripts need a real library ecosystem, **PyO3 is the only engine that brings one wholesale**, and you pay for it in boot time and bundle size. Inside the Lua family, ecosystem reuse and sandboxing pull in opposite directions. PUC Lua and LuaJIT can load LuaRocks (C rocks only through an unsafe state), while Luau's sandbox comes precisely from shutting that door. Pick by which one you need more.

## Conclusion

The common framing, fast-booting interpreters for CLIs and JIT engines for long-running apps, mostly falls apart in Rust. **Half the field boots within 0.3 ms of native**, LuaJIT included. The engines that boot slowly do so for one specific, sometimes avoidable reason (a Scheme prelude, a default-module Context, an uncached Cranelift compile), not because of their execution model. Once you clear the outliers, the decision rests almost entirely on throughput, interop cost and isolation guarantees. On those three axes, mlua's Lua family and wasmtime beat the pure-Rust languages by an order of magnitude or more.

Picking a pure-Rust language is therefore a call about **ergonomics, safety and toolchain**, not speed. Rhai, Starlark and Rune earn their place through Rust-native embedding, op limits, determinism or async, and you pay 3 to 30x in compute for it. Two things are still unmeasured and could shift the middle of the table: **Rhai with the `grain` feature enabled**, and **Roto plus a snapshotted deno_core on the same M5 harness**. Both are worth a rerun before committing to Rhai or JS for anything hot.
